Why Outdoor Work

I’ve spent 11+ years as a UX/UI designer — much of it researching how people use complex systems and explaining what I learn clearly to people who aren’t specialists. Alongside that, I hold a Master’s degree in Landscape Architecture, so land, water, planting, and drainage aren’t new to me; they’re the other half of what I trained in.

Ranch and outdoor work appeals to me because it is direct. Things need to be fixed, cleaned, carried, checked, prepared, maintained, and cared for. I respect that steady work, and I think the same instinct that made me good at explaining a system to a client will make me good at explaining a trail, a watershed, or a piece of wildlife behavior to a guest.

Guiding and interpretation are newer ground for me than grounds work is — I haven’t led hikes professionally yet, but I learn fast and I’m genuinely drawn to it. I’m not looking for a temporary adventure. I’m looking for a team, a property, and a season or longer where I can show up consistently, learn the systems, and be useful.
